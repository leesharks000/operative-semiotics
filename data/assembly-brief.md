## I. THE VISION — everything operative, unfolding from a centre

Every element proceeds out from a centre, and every element is operative: **downloadable, clickable, copyable, executable**. With schematics.

That is not a layout instruction. It is the discipline applied to its own surface. Operative semiotics studies signs that intervene in the systems that read them; a site for it whose elements merely *describe* would be a performative contradiction, which is the title of Notebook I. So the site must be an instance of its subject:

- **downloadable** — the canonical text, at its content address. A changed text is a changed address.
- **clickable** — every reference resolves. A locator that does not point at where a thing may be located is a promise, not an address.
- **copyable** — citation and AXN, to clipboard. The sign travels.
- **executable** — where the element *is* a specification, dataset, score or algorithm, it runs. This one is discriminated, not decorative: a theoretical paper is not executable and must not claim to be.

The centre is the discipline itself. Five rings out: **the work · what constructs it · what it operates with · what it makes · where it meets the world.** The ring order is an argument — Phase X sits in the second ring because the Marx reconstruction is *upstream* of the discipline, not beside it.


### The governing analogy

> **semantic economy : operative semiotics :: political economy : praxis**

This fixes three things. **Which half is which** — semantic economy analyses, operative semiotics intervenes. **The order of construction** — praxis does not follow political economy as application follows theory; the *critique* produces the ground on which praxis becomes thinkable, and just so Phase X produces operative semiotics. And **what it forbids** — praxis is not all action. Activity without theory is mere activity; theory without action is contemplation. The test is narrow: *does the document's semiotic claim do the thing it describes?*
