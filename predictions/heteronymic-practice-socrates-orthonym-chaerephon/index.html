Heteronymy after Pessoa: Socrates as orthonym and the Chaerephon problem

These claims place Pessoa at the height of a describable practice with Kierkegaard and Machado as precursors and parallels, and read the Socratic-Platonic-Aristotelian corpus as one heteronymic configuration with Socrates as the orthonym who does not write. The Chaerephon Problem argues that institutional scholarship cannot tell multi-author transmission from mature heteronymy, that the method has no internal halting criterion, and that repeated philological failure is how the configuration is passed on. Related claims extend the reading to the New Testament (Paul's 'I' as a heteronym's backstory, John as aperture) and treat heteronymy as an ethical practice against reduction to the civil name.
