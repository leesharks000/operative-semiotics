Proxies and priors: how gatekeeping regimes filter what can be read

These claims hold that knowledge evaluation has always run on proxies (journal prestige, citation counts, affiliation) that calcified into gatekeeping, and that copying economies, the Book-of-the-Month Club, modernist legibility demands and peer review installed relevance and legibility regimes long before algorithms. Their present-day versions are classifiers that read literary direct address as prompt injection, a source-relative barrier that keeps an excluded source from correcting the prior that excluded it, and mediation whose losses register as improvement on every metric an institution watches. Forecasts include a market for pre-mediation text, an adversarial arms race against the Mediation Index and rising correction costs against high-prior flattening.
