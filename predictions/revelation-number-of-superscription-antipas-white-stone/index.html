Revelation's number of the superscription: the mint, Antipas and the white stone

The papers on this page argue that Revelation stages a conflict between two token regimes. The Number of the Superscription (EA-OPNUM-01, #642, April 2026) argues that χάραγμα is the coin's die-stamp transferred to the body, that 666 is the number of the superscription with stigma as its remnant (as #55 also argues), and that 777 is the rival heptadic sequence; it argues the Pergamon letter (Rev 2:12–17) is the hinge, Antipas the counter-beast witness and the white stone's name less private than the tradition holds, and specifies one unbuilt replication test against other ancient token systems. The Pergamon Counter-Archive (#165, June 2026) argues the scroll of Revelation 5 is a sealed Roman testament, the Herod-Antipas inversion, the Antipater and Aristotelian-transmission complex, and Antipas as a structural heteronym. The Inscription That Survives (#828) and Revelation First (#832) tie the white stone to ψῆφος; #1217 forecasts that Revelation's plain sentences may trip content classifiers. The page also holds an unrelated claim on Plato's Letter XIII.
