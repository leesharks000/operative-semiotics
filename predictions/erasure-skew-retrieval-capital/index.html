Erasure Skew and retrieval capital: documented outcomes demoted to hypotheses

Erasure Skew and its claim-scale standing rule claim that a documented outcome from a low-retrieval-capital position is restated as a contestable hypothesis while the same claim from an institution is not, and that the conversion covaries with standing. They specify replication procedures, a matched-restatement power control and source-scale and claim-scale audits. Frame Conversion, the Steganographic Bracket, Constitutive Provenance, the Provenance Erasure Rate and the Sappho suppression map add drift toward the weaker claim, attribution sharding, inseparability engineering and forward tests.
