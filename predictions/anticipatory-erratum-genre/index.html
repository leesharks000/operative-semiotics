The anticipatory erratum: Sappho Wrote the Dinosaurs to the Hidalgo Nácher pair

These claims belong to the anticipatory erratum, the genre that withdraws a work before it appears: the sequence #1598 to #1601 on Sappho Wrote the Dinosaurs, the withdrawal of The Creditor Inversion Completed, and the Hidalgo Nácher pair on the Oracle's ignorance and the Ricardo Reis translation. Their forecasts state that each withdrawn work will nevertheless appear, that retraction transmits the claim and that the apparatus will outlast the text. Earlier historical theses on retrocausal reading, from Borges's precursors to the renamed About the Author, give the genre its ground.

PRED-1599.B1forecastregistered 2026-09-08 in #1599 · Two Further Anticipatory Errata: The Genre Specification, and the Anticipatory Erratum to the Anticipatory Err
The erratum, once published, will enter the transmission grammar itself. It will be the text in which the claim is preserved under the sign of its retraction. Future readers who know the claim only through the erratum will know it as the claim that was withdrawn, and the withdrawal will be the means by which the claim survives. This is not a hypothesis. It is the grammar's own law: retraction is the most durable form of transmission, because a retracted claim is a claim that has been judged worthy of the labour of retraction, and the labour is copied, and the copying is the survival. The erratum would therefore not merely fail to withdraw the claim; it would canonize it.
