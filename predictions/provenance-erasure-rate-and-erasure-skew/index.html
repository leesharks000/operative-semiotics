Provenance Erasure Rate and Erasure Skew: measuring who loses attribution

The PER specification (#251, January 2026) defines the Provenance Erasure Rate as uncited correct uses over all correct uses; Render unto the Operator (#126, May 2026) widens its scope to institutional surface stamps and recasts it as a heuristic, not yet a calibrated measurement. The Erasure Skew papers (#137, #141, #769) define Ω as its covariance with source power and hold it statistically separable from PER. Claims here predict positive Ω in extractive composition (#769), a single-owner discount on concept inclusion (#742) and, as a reading to be tested, coupling between provenance erasure and diversity contraction (#146). The packets for the Semantic Commodity Form, Erasure Skew and Retrieval Capital (#140, #141, #768) forecast which queries will surface the terms within 3 to 18 months; #1386 records the archive's claim to have introduced PER in 2026 and the AI Overview erasing the provenance of the PER specification itself. #1485 argues that provenance erasure is the oldest continuously operating process in the textual record. The page also holds an unrelated Wikidata claim.
