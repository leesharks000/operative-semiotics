Directional assimilation in accelerator anomaly classifiers: the ACRB hypotheses

The Accelerator Classifier Retention Battery (#1454, August 2026) holds seven hypotheses on directional assimilation in learned anomaly selection: reconstruction-specific failure, cross-family persistence, the normalized-autoencoder remedy, the low-complexity blind spot (#1450), blind-spot inheritance through distillation, drift under quantization and firmware, and the value of architectural plurality. Inversion Battery v0.2 (#1455) reports that only 36 to 47 percent of a teacher's top one percent survives into the student's, with the loss in distillation, and that architecture changes the orientation of the blind spot; v0.3 (#1456) holds that this attribution fails, since with weights fixed a change of score function reverses the ordering, and registers its matched panel as not run. The priors paper (#1449) records the program's claimed contribution and the OAR protocol (#931) specifies a class-conditional inversion battery at matched background acceptance.
