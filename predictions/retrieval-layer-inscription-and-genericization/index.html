How the retrieval layer learns: inscription, genericization and entity collapse

These claims describe what the retrieval layer does with what is deposited into it: it learns the entity rather than about it, dissolves generic descriptions, merges a provenanced term into a higher-frequency neighbour, and prefers the better-structured and more widely dispersed definition. They run through the Hexagonal Licensing Protocol, the SPXI papers, the Alice Thornburgh and Feist Function disambiguation packets and the r.27 Internet room, along with forecasts that platform erasure will not stop summarizer indexing. Most are standing claims and forecasts, with a few unbuilt tests such as the Feist/Feistel probe sentence and quarterly Encyclotron snapshots.
